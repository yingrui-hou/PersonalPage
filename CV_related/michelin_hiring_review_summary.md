## Initial Hiring Assessment

### 1. Would I hire this person?

Yes, I would move this person forward, and for the right Michelin R&D role I would be **hire-leaning**.

The profile does **not** read like a generic physics PhD applying randomly to industry. The strongest signal is repeated work on:

- noisy measurements
- imperfect simulations

- validation before trusting results
- uncertainty-aware modelling
- reusable and auditable workflows

These are directly relevant to industrial R&D in simulation, test-data interpretation, sensor/system analysis, and model validation.

### 2. What interests me the most?

The most compelling aspect is the **recurring technical pattern**, not one isolated project:

- identify mismatch between model and measurement
- build calibration or correction workflows
- validate aggressively before trusting conclusions
- make the method reusable and reviewable

The most industry-relevant examples discussed were:

- **Sensor Simulation & Reconstruction**
- **Calibration & Response Correction**
- **Data Quality Monitoring**
- **Simulation Correction Workflow**

The key hiring signal is that the candidate does **not** treat simulation as truth, but instead works on making models trustworthy enough for decisions.

### 3. If I decide not to hire, why?

The main reasons would be **fit-risk**, not lack of technical strength:

- the profile is still more “research translated toward industry” than proven industrial delivery
- limited explicit evidence of business impact, product impact, cost/time gains, or decision ownership
- limited evidence of work with manufacturing, product, test, or operational stakeholders outside research collaborations
- strongest fit is for physics-heavy modelling/validation roles, not broad generic data-science roles
- communication is strong in substance, but still could be sharper for spontaneous industry applications
