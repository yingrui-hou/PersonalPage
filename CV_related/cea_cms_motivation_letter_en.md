Dear Members of the Selection Committee,

I am writing to apply for the permanent Research Engineer position in high-energy particle physics within the CMS group of the Department of Particle Physics at IRFU, CEA Paris-Saclay. I hold a PhD in particle physics and have developed my recent work through postdoctoral research within the CNRS, CERN, and the LHCb collaboration. My profile has been shaped at the interface of data analysis, detector validation, simulation, and instrumentation-oriented R&D. This combination is precisely why I am strongly interested in joining the Saclay CMS group for the LS3, HL-LHC, and future-collider program.

In my recent work, I developed reusable C++/Python/ROOT workflows for weak-signal extraction, time-dependent inference, calibration, and validation on noisy datasets, often in the absence of direct event-level truth. I implemented likelihood-based models, joint fits, simulation-to-data alignment through reweighting and control samples, and ML-based selection workflows validated under dataset shift. This experience trained me to turn imperfect measurements and incomplete models into robust, traceable, and physically interpretable results.

At the same time, I carried out detector-response and measurement-chain studies with Geant4/C++, combining beam-test data, response maps, and simulation. I quantified the impact of detector non-uniformity on calorimeter resolution, studied how timing information can improve reconstructed energy, and built validation workflows that led to the identification of detector and software issues later addressed in reconstruction. These projects gave me practical experience connecting instrumentation, reconstruction, calibration, and physics analysis, which I see as directly relevant to the missions of this position.

The scientific environment of IRFU, and especially the DPhP CMS group, is closely aligned with my interests. The combination of advanced CMS analyses, detector upgrade and commissioning work for the HL-LHC, and detector-performance studies for future colliders provides exactly the type of long-term framework in which I want to work. I am particularly drawn to Saclay's position at the interface of Higgs physics, precision calibration of electromagnetic objects, and instrumentation development. The IRFU highlight published on April 2, 2026, on the CMS Higgs-boson mass measurement in the diphoton channel, enabled by new photon calibration techniques developed at IRFU, illustrates the kind of contribution I want to make: methodological and detector-level advances with direct impact on precision physics results.

Although my main collaboration experience has been with LHCb rather than CMS, the skills I built are immediately transferable: analysis and simulation software development, rigorous validation in large collaborations, interpretation of complex datasets, review culture, and the ability to connect experimental constraints to physics goals. I want to bring this experience to the Saclay CMS group by contributing to analyses robust against detector and modelling effects, to the preparation of detector operation after LS3, to subsystem-performance validation, and to reusable tools for the HL-LHC era.

Beyond direct scientific contributions, I want to invest in the collective life of the department through student supervision, software and validation best practices, teaching, scientific outreach, and participation in the group's long-term strategy. I care strongly about collaborative work, software quality, experimental rigor, and the safety constraints associated with laboratory and site activities. I work daily in scientific English and continue strengthening my French so that I can participate fully in the life of the laboratory.

Joining CEA Paris-Saclay would be a natural next step for me: to continue doing high-energy physics in a leading environment while contributing durably to a group recognized for linking analysis, detector expertise, and long-term scientific strategy.
